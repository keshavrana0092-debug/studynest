How to avoid rental scams in Australia: a guide for international students

StudyNest Renting Guide · 6 minute read

Finding a room from overseas is stressful, and scammers know it. Fake rental listings often target international students who cannot inspect a property in person. This guide explains the most common warning signs, the checks to do before you pay, and what to do if something goes wrong.

In this guide
  1. Warning signs of a rental scam
  2. Six checks before you pay anything
  3. How rental bonds work in Victoria

1. Warning signs of a rental scam

  • The rent is much lower than similar rooms in the same suburb. A deal that looks too good to be true usually is.
  • The “landlord” is overseas and says they cannot show you the property, but will post the keys after you pay.
  • You are pressured to pay quickly to “secure” the room because “many other students are interested”.
  • Unusual payment methods are requested, such as gift cards, cryptocurrency or money transfer services instead of a traceable bank payment.
  • The photos appear elsewhere online under a different address or name.
  • The person avoids video calls or gives vague answers about the address, bills or lease.
Golden rule: never send money for a room you have not seen, in person or on a live video call, and never pay someone whose identity you cannot confirm.

2. Six checks before you pay anything

  1. Do a live video inspection. Ask the host to walk through the room, kitchen and bathroom, and to show the street outside.
  2. Confirm the address. Look it up on a map and check that the street view matches the listing.
  3. Search the photos and the name. A reverse image search can reveal photos copied from other listings.
  4. Ask for a written agreement. A genuine host or agent will provide a lease or room agreement that states the rent, bond and bills.
  5. Ask how the bond will be handled. In Victoria, bonds must be lodged with the RTBA (see below).
  6. Use a protected payment method. Platforms like StudyNest hold your payment until you move in, so you are never paying a stranger directly.

3. How rental bonds work in Victoria

A bond is a security deposit that protects the landlord if there is damage or unpaid rent at the end of your tenancy. In Victoria, the bond is not kept by the landlord. It must be lodged with the Residential Tenancies Bond Authority (RTBA), and you should receive confirmation once it has been lodged. At the end of your tenancy, the bond is returned through the RTBA.

Before you move in, you should also receive a condition report describing the state of the property. Check it carefully, add your own notes and photos, and keep a copy. It helps you get your bond back when you leave.

For official and up-to-date information, visit Consumer Affairs Victoria (consumer.vic.gov.au) and the RTBA (rentalbonds.vic.gov.au).

4. What to do if you have been scammed

  1. Contact your bank immediately. They may be able to stop or reverse the payment.
  2. Stop all contact with the scammer and do not send more money.
  3. Report it to Scamwatch (scamwatch.gov.au) and to the website or social media group where you found the listing.
  4. Tell your university. International student support services can help you with emergency accommodation and advice.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do if I think a rental listing is a scam?

Stop all contact and do not send any money. If you have already paid, contact your bank immediately, then report the scam to Scamwatch and to the website where you found the listing.

Where is my rental bond kept in Victoria?

In Victoria, rental bonds are lodged with the Residential Tenancies Bond Authority (RTBA), not kept by the landlord. You should receive confirmation from the RTBA once your bond has been lodged.
